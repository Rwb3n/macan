# Gap scan: the firm side, prices and coverage for UK planning surveys

Date: 2026-10-23. Sources were gathered with Firecrawl search and scrape (about 34 calls). Evidence only. People are named by role, not by name. Where something was not found, this file says so.

## 1. Capacity and season

- **CIEEM (the professional body) on shortages, 2021.** It describes a "severe shortage of skilled and experienced ecologists", a shortage of applicants "especially at senior levels", and survey-season workloads that are "unacceptably heavy". It says development is "at serious risk of delays". https://cieem.net/a-crisis-in-our-sector/
- **CIEEM State of the Profession 2025.** The survey ran in Jan/Feb 2025, "at a time of ongoing concern about capacity in our profession". Mandatory BNG "has created a lot of work pressure and frustration in the short term". https://cieem.net/resource/cieem-state-of-the-profession-survey-report-2025/ and https://cieem.net/cieem-publishes-2025-state-of-the-profession-survey-report/ (I did not open the PDF, so I have no percentages.)
- **Council-side shortage.** An Idox article says 79% of planning authorities report recruitment difficulties, and 89% cite a lack of candidates with the required skills (Feb 2026). https://idoxgeospatial.co.uk/2026/02/18/englands-planning-ecology-crisis/
- **Firms booked out.**
  - An ecological consultancy posted on Instagram: "fully booked until September 2025". https://www.instagram.com/p/DMvuy56NWfI/
  - A landscape and ecology firm posted on X: "fully booked for May bat surveys back in…". https://x.com/LizardLD
  - PJC posted on Facebook: "booking up fast for the upcoming bat survey season". https://www.facebook.com/pjcconsultancy/posts/this-brown-long-eared-bat-was-recently-found-in-a-mortise-joint-by-one-of-our-li/687335096728153/
- **What firms say on their own sites about the season.**
  - Veon (Scotland) says bat ecologists are "in high demand during the summer". It says late-season evenings "can become heavily booked" and a late enquiry "may leave only a small number of possible dates". If the season is missed, the survey "may need to wait until the following year". https://veonecology.co.uk/the-bat-survey-season-is-moving-quickly-why-development-teams-should-act-now/
  - Teylu says "Survey slots fill up quickly in peak season". https://www.teylu-ecology.co.uk/blog-1/blog-post-title-one-fcrbj
  - Alyne gives a report lead time of 2–3 working days after the survey, with a May–Sept season. https://www.alyneecology.co.uk/species-survey/bats/bat-emergence-surveys/
  - **Not found:** a booking lead time stated in weeks on any firm's site.
- **Seasonal hiring and subcontracting.** Firms add capacity with temporary and freelance surveyors.
  - Indeed lists 238 "ecologist bat survey" jobs in England and 298 "seasonal habitat ecology" jobs (counts are as Indeed showed them, undated). https://uk.indeed.com/q-ecologist-bat-survey-l-england-jobs.html and https://uk.indeed.com/q-seasonal-habitat-ecology-jobs.html
  - Richard Green Ecology advertises part-time May–Sept bat-survey roles. https://www.richardgreenecology.co.uk/seasonal-ecologist-vacancies-2/
  - Arbtech says many of its surveys are done by "subcontract and/or part-time bat survey assistants". https://arbtech.co.uk/bat-survey-assistants/ and https://talents.studysmarter.co.uk/companies/arbtech/freelance-ecologist-bat-surveyor-for-bat-emergence-and-re-entry-surveys-bers-10798882/
  - A small firm says it "tended to subcontract out most of our bat work… using… other friendly local consultancies and freelancers". https://www.cgoecology.com/blog/cgo-ecology-news?start=20
- **Counter-evidence: demand is not uniformly high.**
  - The FT (Mar 2025) reports that "UK planning reforms threaten thousands of ecologists' jobs". https://www.ft.com/content/7a971bac-981d-447d-853e-d215f183594d
  - SearchFlow reports large planning applications down 23% in Q1 2025, with small applications stable. https://www.searchflow.co.uk/news-and-events/large-planning-applications-down-23-in-q1-2025/
- **Do small firms turn work away?** Not found as an explicit statement; my search for "turning work away" returned nothing. "Fully booked" posts are the closest evidence.

## 2. How small firms win work today

- **Direct sales to the people who specify surveys.** Elton Ecology was hiring a Sales and Marketing Administrator (posted 14 Oct 2026) to phone "architects, planning consultants, property developers", against "activity targets". This shows a firm actively looking for work. https://elton-ecology.co.uk/home/careers/
- **Referrals from architects and planners.**
  - Peak Ecology lists "planning consultants, architects and landscape architects" among its clients. https://www.peakecology.co.uk/
  - Ecology by Design's testimonials come from an architect practice and a planning manager, and one says they first met "on Twitter". https://www.ecologybydesign.co.uk/
- **Selling capacity to other consultancies.** Aware Ecology says "We also offer ecology subcontracting services to undertake bat surveys". https://www.awareecology.co.uk/survey-information/bat-surveys
- **The professional body's directory.** CIEEM runs a Registered Practices Directory for clients to find a consultancy. https://cieem.net/i-need/finding-a-consultant/
- **The national firm also refers work on.** Arbtech passes noise and air-quality enquiries to partner firms (see section 4).
- **Lead sites.**
  - Generic Bark complaints are plentiful, for example "FAKE LEADS" (G2), and themes of bad leads, no refund and expired credits (Hellopeter). https://www.g2.com/products/bark-com/reviews?page=9&qs=pros-and-cons and https://www.hellopeter.com/barkcom
  - Worklanded's bat-survey lead page quotes Bark at £1.80 per credit and 5–20+ credits per lead, shared with up to 5 firms. Worklanded itself charges £26 per lead with no contract. https://worklanded.com/leads/bat-surveys
  - **Not found:** an ecology or planning-survey firm specifically complaining about Bark or any other lead site.
- **Marketing forums for ecology firms.** Not found.

**Benchmark ranges (guides, not firms' own prices).**
- PRA: average £500, range £400–600 (Checkatrade). https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/cost-guides/bat-survey-cost/
- Planning noise survey: £1,450–2,500 + VAT (a firm's 2026 guide). https://www.murrayacoustics.com/insights/noise-survey-cost-uk
- Noise mechanical-plant assessment: about £1,000 (2021). https://www.aca-acoustics.co.uk/uncategorized/how-much-does-a-noise-survey-cost/

**Is local cheaper? Mixed.**
- **PRA:** three small English firms publish lower "from" prices than Arbtech (£299, £350, £375 vs £399). Crossman's £595/£895 are packages of unstated scope, so they are left out of this comparison.
- **Emergence surveys:** the South West firm prices 2 emergence surveys at about £800 + VAT (a 2019 page). That looks like half Arbtech's "from £799", but the scope of Arbtech's £799 (one visit or a set) is not stated. Crossman's per-visit price (£525) is below £799.
- **Scotland and Northern Ireland:** local firms are dearer (PEA £850–900 vs Arbtech £599; NI emergence from £2,400 for 3 visits).
- **FRA:** the lowest published prices (£295–£450) undercut Arbtech's £599 hub figure.
- **Caveat:** these are all "from" prices, and the scope behind Arbtech's £799 (one visit or a set) is not stated.

## 4. Arbtech coverage holes

- **Surveys done by partners, not in-house.**
  - **Noise:** "For any other services we don't cover… connecting with… partner companies for referring… The acoustic consultants from the external consultancy…" https://arbtech.co.uk/noise-survey-assessment/
  - **Air quality:** "we can then speak to our air quality partner and create a reflective quote". It also mentions a money-back guarantee on local authority acceptance. https://arbtech.co.uk/air-quality-assessment/
  - **Contaminated land** (from £950): the page says "geo-environmental consultants at Arbtech". I could not tell whether this work is in-house or done by a partner. https://arbtech.co.uk/contaminated-land/
  - **Flood risk:** the hub lists "from £599". In-house or partner: not found. https://arbtech.co.uk/flood-risk-water-and-drainage/
- **Regions.**
  - The bat-survey page says "all over England and Wales". https://arbtech.co.uk/protected-species/bat-surveys/
  - Scotland is claimed elsewhere: an Edinburgh page, and a case study saying "England, Scotland and Wales". https://arbtech.co.uk/bat-survey-edinburgh/ and https://arbtech.co.uk/case-studies/north-wales-bat-survey/
  - **Northern Ireland:** no service-coverage claim found. Arbtech pages mention the NI regulator only in general text. https://arbtech.co.uk/protected-species/
- **Turnaround claims.**
  - "Enquiries responded to in hours, not days. Surveys in days, not weeks." https://arbtech.co.uk/
  - A paid upgrade guarantees the report "by 5pm the day after your survey". https://arbtech.co.uk/
  - The report is released "when payment is made". https://arbtech.co.uk/
- **Capacity dependency.** Arbtech relies on subcontract and part-time bat surveyors in season (see section 1).

## Gaps this suggests (testable guesses)

1. **Small firms are full in season but have spare capacity the rest of the year.** "Fully booked" posts cluster in May–Sept, while firms like Elton phone architects for work. Test: ask 10 small firms whether they would pay for Oct–Apr enquiries (PRA, PEA, BNG baseline, pre-booking next season's emergence surveys) and whether they would turn May–Aug leads down.
2. **Overflow routing between firms.** Booked-out firms already subcontract to "friendly local consultancies", and Aware Ecology advertises subcontract capacity. Test: ask 5 booked-out firms whether they would pass overflow enquiries to a vetted local firm for a referral fee, and ask 5 firms with free dates whether they would take them.
3. **Noise and air quality are under-supplied by the national brand.** Arbtech refers both to partners, so independent acoustic and air-quality consultancies are a reachable seller group that is not tied to Arbtech. Test: call 5 acoustic consultancies about lead appetite and price per lead, against a £1,450–2,500 job size.
4. **Northern Ireland, and possibly Scotland, are thinly served.** Arbtech makes no NI claim, and local NI and Glasgow prices are higher. Test: count CIEEM Registered Practices and Google-listed firms per NI and Scottish council area, and check whether NI homeowners' enquiries go unanswered.
5. **Price-transparent local firms beat Arbtech on headline PRA and FRA prices.** Small firms publish £299–£375 for a PRA and £295–£450 for an FRA. A directory that shows fixed local prices could win homeowners from Arbtech. Test: on a landing page, compare enquiry conversion with and without published local "from" prices.
